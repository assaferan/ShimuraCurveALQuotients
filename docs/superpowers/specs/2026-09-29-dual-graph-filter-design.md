# Dual-graph filter at p | D: design

Status: design, 2026-09-29. Prototype and evidence:
`handoff_2026-09-29/graph-pilot/` (pilot, Stankewicz-based) and the session scratchpad
`design/` (this rebuild: `dg2.m`, `t1b.log`, `t2b.log`, `t3.log`, `t5.log`, `big3.log`, `big4.log`).

## 1. Purpose and theorem

Y = X_0^D(N)/W, D > 1, N squarefree, gcd(D, N) = 1, p | D prime.

* Y over Z_p is a Mumford curve. The dual graph of its stable special fibre is the minimised
  quotient graph G_W, with edge lengths given by stabilizer orders (PadurariuâSaia,
  arXiv:2509.25368, Thm 2.6, after Ogg 1985, *Mauvaise rÃ©duction des courbes de Shimura*, and
  BertoliniâDarmon 1996).
* Specialization (Baker 2008, *Specialization of linear systems from curves to graphs*):
  gon(Î) â¤ gon(Y).
* A 2-edge-connected metric graph of genus â¥ 2 has gonality 2 iff it has an involutive isometry
  whose quotient is a tree (BakerâNorine 2009, *Harmonic morphisms and hyperelliptic graphs*;
  Chan 2013, *Tropical hyperelliptic curves*). The quotient is a tree iff
  Ï(Fix Î¹) = g + 1.

**If the canonical model of G_W has no such involution for some p | D, then Y is not
hyperelliptic.** The test only works in that direction: a hyperelliptic graph proves nothing.

We use only the **unweighted** variant, which puts every edge at length 1 after
canonicalisation. An isometric involution with tree quotient is in particular a combinatorial
automorphism with the same fixed set. So if no combinatorial involution exists, no isometric one
exists either, whatever the lengths are. The pilot measured the cost of this choice at about 5%
of the yield (165 against 174 open curves ruled out). In exchange, no unit groups or stabilizer
orders are ever computed.

## 2. Scope

The filter applies to curves with D > 1, N squarefree and g â¥ 3. Curves with D = 1 or with N
not squarefree are skipped and left untouched: no attribute is set, and the stage prints a count
`DualGraph: n not applicable (D = 1 or N not squarefree)`. On the committed data, 517 undecided
curves with g â¥ 3 at `UpdateCurves2` are not applicable, and 1550 are.

## 3. Data layer: `DualGraph.m`

A new package file, `DualGraph.m`, is listed in `ShimuraQuotients.spec` after
`special_fiber_modular.m`. The `.sig` file is generated by Magma and is already ignored by
`.gitignore` (`*.sig`).

### Magma API findings (experiments in `design/`)

* `BrandtModule(D', M)` and `BrandtModule(O)` expose **no ideal representatives**.
  `ListSignatures(ModBrdt)` (`sigs.log`) has no ideal accessor and no degeneracy maps. The
  attribute `LeftIdeals` exists but is never assigned by either constructor (`attrs.log`,
  `ordmod.log`). The two constructors also order their bases differently: w_2 differs between
  `BrandtModule(3,10)` and `BrandtModule(QuaternionOrder(Q,10))`. So a Brandt basis cannot be
  aligned with `LeftIdealClasses`, and the origin map cannot come from Brandt modules.
* `AtkinLehnerOperator(M, q)` works for q | D' and for q | level, including q = p on the level-Np
  module. It returns signed permutation matrices: uniform sign â1 at ramified q and +1 at level
  q in every case observed. `M`AtkinLehnerPermutations` caches the permutations.
* **Brandt modules are slow.** `BrandtModule(3, 1610)` takes 232 s (`prof2.log`). The whole
  graph construction below takes 10â12 s at that level. Brandt modules are therefore used only
  as a test-time cross-check (Â§7).
* The Brandt matrix B(p) at level N counts edges weighted by 1/|Aut|, and it has no edge labels.
  The action of w_q on individual edges cannot be recovered from it, so this route was rejected.

### Construction (prototype `design/dg2.m`)

    Q := QuaternionAlgebra(D div p);  O := QuaternionOrder(Q, N);  OO := Order(O, p);
    L := LeftIdealClasses(O);          // vertices, h of them per copy
    LL := LeftIdealClasses(OO);        // unoriented edges, h' of them

1. **Class lookup.** The key of a left ideal J is
   `MinkowskiGramReduction(GramMatrix(J)/Norm(J) : Canonical := true)`. This is invariant under
   J â¦ JÎ±. Every lookup is confirmed with `IsIsomorphic`. If no confirmed match is found in the
   key bucket, the lookup falls back to a scan over all classes, and it asserts exactly one hit.
   So a key that is not canonical costs time, never correctness. The key separated all
   288 classes at level (3, 770), and no fallback fired in any run. An isomorphism test costs
   about 0.5 ms. Bucketing by right-order type alone, as `fiber` does, gives buckets of 8â32
   and was 3Ã slower.
2. **Origin map.** org[e] = class of `lideal<O | Basis(LL[e])>` in L.
3. **AL action.** For each prime q | D'Np, P := `PrimeIdeal(OO, q)` (asserted two-sided, of
   norm q), and alE[q] is the permutation e â¦ class(PÂ·LL[e]). For q â  p, alV[q] is defined the
   same way on L with `PrimeIdeal(O, q)`. Each map is asserted to be an involution, and
   equivariance org(e^alE[q]) = org(e)^alV[q] is asserted for every q â  p. Composite w_m is the
   product of the prime permutations, which commute because two-sided ideals do.
4. **Graph G.** The vertices are (v, Â±), v â [1..h]. Edge e joins (org[e], +) to
   (org[e^alE[p]], â). The group W acts as follows: w_q with q â  p acts by alV[q] on vertices,
   keeping the sign, and by alE[q] on edges. w_p swaps the sign with the label fixed, and acts
   by alE[p] on edges. This is exactly the corrected ("Aprime") reading of
   `bipartite_cover_fiber` (pilot README, "the bug found").

Intrinsic:

    intrinsic DualGraphData(D::RngIntElt, N::RngIntElt, p::RngIntElt) -> Tup
    {<h, h', org, alE, alV> for X_0^D(N) at p | D; cached in memory and on disk.}

The intrinsic asserts h' â 2h + 1 = `GenusShimuraCurveQuotient(D, N, {1})` before returning.

### Cache

Caching follows the repo's two conventions:

* In memory: a new store `dual_graphs := NewStore();` in `Caching.m` (next to `cached_traces`,
  `Caching.m:2`), used through `GetCache`/`SetCache` (`Caching.m:48â66`) with key `<D, N, p>`.
* On disk: `data/dualgraph/dualgraph_v1_<D>_<N>_<p>`. `v1` is a format stamp: bump it whenever the
  construction or the tuple layout changes, since validation cannot detect data that is
  consistent but stale. The file is a Magma-evaluable
  `Sprint(<h, h', org, [<q, Eltseq(alE[q])>], [<q, Eltseq(alV[q])>]>, "Magma")`. It is read with
  `eval Read` when `FileExists`, as in `polymake_solution_*` (`BorcherdsForms.m:212â217`). Each
  file is written to `<name>.tmp` and then renamed with `System("mv ...")`, so a killed worker
  cannot leave a truncated file. The involution, equivariance and genus assertions are re-run
  on every disk read, so a corrupt file raises an error instead of giving a verdict.
* **Labels are not canonical.** `LeftIdealClasses` returns its representatives in a different
  order on each call (measured: two builds of (6, 35, 2) give different `org`). Cached data is
  therefore one labelling of the graph, and tests compare two builds only up to isomorphism, via
  the quotient graphs for every W in `ALSubgroups(DN)`, never with `eq`.

## 4. Quotient graph for W (pure combinatorics)

* Vertex orbits: orbits of W on the 2h vertices.
* Edge orbits: orbits of W on [1..h']. An orbit is a **half-edge**, and is dropped, iff some
  m â W with p | m fixes e. Such an m swaps the two ends of e, because they lie in opposite
  copies. For p â¤ m, fixing e preserves its orientation.
* Each remaining orbit gives one edge between the vertex orbits of its two ends. Leaves are
  kept at this step, since they do not affect b_1.
* **Genus assertion on every call.** b_1 = #E â #V(used) + 1 must equal `X`g`. If it does not,
  `error "DUALGRAPH_GENUS: ..."` names (D, N, W, p). This stops the stage deliberately, as
  `BADDIM` does for the twisted stages (`worktrees/integration/docs/RUNNING_PIPELINE.md`,
  "A stage that stops on purpose").

## 5. Canonical model and involution search

These are ported from `handoff_2026-09-29/graph-pilot/graphtest.m:17â140`.

* `canonical`: repeat until stable. Delete a leaf (a vertex of valence 1 that is not a loop).
  Contract a bridge, detected by a connectivity test with that edge removed. Merge the two edges
  at a valence-2 vertex without loops. Relabel. The result is 2-edge-connected with all
  valences â¥ 3, and the genus is unchanged; the implementation asserts this.
* Lengths are then set to 1 (the unweighted variant).
* `search`: backtracking over involutions Ï of the vertices. Assign the lowest unassigned u to
  itself or to some unassigned v > u. Prune as soon as some assigned pair (a, b) has an edge
  multiplicity different from that of (Ï a, Ï b). At a full assignment, compute Ï(Fix):
    * fixed vertices;
    * plus the loops at fixed vertices;
    * minus, for each pair of distinct fixed vertices a, b, the parity of mult(a, b), since
      paired parallel edges swap and an odd one is left fixed;
    * plus mult(a, b) for each swapped pair a â b, since each such edge is reversed and its
      midpoint is fixed.

  Assert Ï â¤ g + 1. The graph is hyperelliptic iff Ï = g + 1.
* The prototype searches with Ï on vertices only, which fixes Ï on edges only up to
  permutations inside each parallel class. The count above is correct for that. Per-quotient
  cost was under 0.01 s on every pilot curve (`t2b.log`, field `t=`).

## 6. Verdict recording and pipeline placement

`FilterByDualGraph(~curves)` follows `FilterByTrace` (`ShimuraQuotients.m:773â787`):

    if assigned X`IsSubhyp then continue; end if;          // never overwrite
    ... for p in the ordered primes of D:
        if not is_hyperelliptic_graph(...) then
            curves[i]`IsSubhyp := false;  curves[i]`IsHyp := false;
            curves[i]`TestInWhichProved := Sprintf("DualGraph at p = %o", p);  break;

**p = 2 policy.** The filter uses every p | D, as the theorem allows. It tries the odd primes
first, in ascending order, and p = 2 last. So "DualGraph at p = 2" appears exactly on the
verdicts that rely on p = 2 alone, and the stage summary prints that count. In the pilot this
was 35 of 165 (165 unweighted against 130 using odd p only).

**Phase A.** Stage `FilterByDualGraphStar` goes directly after `FilterByTraceStar`
(`worktrees/integration/run_pipeline.sh:122`), but after the check-only `HHProposition1`
(`:126`). That stage reads `curves_after_FilterByTraceStar.dat` by name, and `VerifyHHTable2`
checks it. HH's curves are D = 1, so they are not applicable here anyway. Changes:

* `run_parallel_filter.sh:32â44`: the input of `FilterByDualGraphStar` is
  `curves_after_HHProposition1.dat`, and `FilterByTwistedTraceStar` now reads
  `curves_after_FilterByDualGraphStar.dat`.
* Phase A has no closure stage. Like the other star stages, its verdicts reach the covers
  through `GetQuotientsAndGenera` and `UpdateCurves1`.

**Phase B.** Stage `FilterByDualGraph` goes directly after `UpdateCurves2`
(`run_pipeline.sh:162`). It is followed by the closure `UpdateCurvesAfterDualGraph`, added to
the `UpdateCurves(~curves)` case in `run_sequential_stage.m:62â66`. `Genus3CoversGenus2` then
reads `curves_after_UpdateCurvesAfterDualGraph.dat`. The naming follows the "Stage order" rule:
existing `UpdateCurves<N>` names are not renumbered.

**Parallel, by level.** Add both stages to the level-grouped set in
`parallel_filter_worker.m:80â81`, so that a level's fibers are built once per chunk. Add
`star_of["FilterByDualGraph"] := "FilterByDualGraphStar"` (`:55â58`), since star curves need not
be re-run. A level's cost proxy is the sum of h'Â·Ï(DN) over p | D, from the mass formula. The

## 7. Tests: `tests/DualGraph.m`

This file follows the `tests/Kappa0.m` pattern: one `procedure test_...()` with `assert`s, called
at the end. It is picked up by `run_tests.m:18` because its name has no leading `_`. It runs with
`target:=DualGraph`.

1. **Genus identities.** This is the pilot's `genustest.m` list: all (curve, p) pairs over the 14
   levels with squarefree N. DÂ·N â¤ 700 is pinned in the test (822 identities, about 25 s), and
   the full set of 988 goes to the offline suite. The rebuild gives **988/988 OK**
   (`design/t1b.log`), including the levels (15,2), (21,2), (26,1), (35,1), (57,1), (546,1)
   and (1974,1). (22, 45) is excluded because N is not squarefree.
2. **K4.** X_0^6(55)/â¨w_3, w_110â© at p = 3 has a canonical model isomorphic to K_4 (tested by
   graph isomorphism after subdividing edges), and it has no tree-quotient involution
   (`design/t3.log`).
3. **Ten known-hyperelliptic curves** from the pilot's `hyp.m`, spread over D, pass at every
   p | D. The pilot passed 122 of 122.
4. **Negative controls.** Each corrupts a data tuple and is run over the 822 identities
   (`design/pkg/mirror/tests/DualGraph.m`). The test asserts that each wrong variant breaks at
   least one identity.
    * w_p made trivial on the edges, so there is no reversal: 431 fail.
    * w_p on the edges replaced by w_q0: 397 fail.
    * a vertex-moving w_q made trivial on the vertices: 279–305 fail, depending on the labelling,
      because this one breaks equivariance.

   A fourth corruption, exchanging two origin labels, breaks only 4–15 identities. So origin-map
   errors are caught by the equivariance assertion in `ValidateDualGraphData`, which is tested
   separately. The pilot's code-level variants (`design/t5.m`) gave 215, 386 and 483 failures for
   no reversal, w_p not swapping the copies, and half-edges kept.
5. **Stankewicz agreement.** Raw `fiber` output (edge list and AL arrays) is copied from the
   pilot as literals for (6,35), (10,33) and (14,5) at every p. The test asserts that the
   un-minimised quotient graph for every W is isomorphic to the one built from those literals.
   The rebuild gives 298 of 298 isomorphic (`design/t3.log`), once isolated vertices, which the
   pilot drops, are ignored.
6. **Brandt cross-check.** At three levels with h' â¤ 150, the fixed-point count of every element
   of the AL group on L and on LL equals the count from `BrandtModule(D', N)` and
   `BrandtModule(D', Np)`. Fixed-point counts are the invariant that survives the unalignable
   bases. The test also asserts that `NonHyperellipticAtPrimeCertificate` never returns a
   DualGraph certificate.

On the full pilot list (`design/t2b.log`, compared by `design/cmp.py`), the rebuild's unweighted
verdicts and canonical (V, E) agree with the pilot on all 805 (curve, p) pairs reached before the
run was stopped (of 1029; the run was stopped for time, not failure). There were no genus mismatches,
and all 421 Brandt checks passed (h' ≤ 150). Over those pairs the tallies are: known
hyperelliptic 0 of 106 ruled out, known non-hyperelliptic 75 of 75, open 124 of 153.
