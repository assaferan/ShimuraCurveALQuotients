## X_0^1326(1): why the plateau is not a verdict against it

Between cutoff 46 and 50 at 30 digits the residual stopped falling -- 9.128e-13 then 9.144e-13 --
while the rest of the spectrum agreed to twelve significant digits (0.021900900036168...,
0.125908476207997...).  A residual that is stable under the cutoff is what a genuine, very small
singular value would look like, which would mean the curve is NOT hyperelliptic, so this had to be
settled rather than assumed.  Raising the working precision from 30 to 50 digits at cutoff 46
drops the residual by a factor of 112, to 8.18e-15, leaving the rest of the spectrum unchanged to
eleven digits.  A genuine singular value does not move with the working precision; a numerical
zero does.  So the residual is zero and the curve is hyperelliptic.

The contrast with a genuine smallest singular value is about as sharp as it could be, and the
non-hyperelliptic control measures it.  At X_0^91(1)/w_7, cutoff 40, the smallest singular value is

    0.0183504187415874290636060502918                      at 30 digits
    0.018350418741587429063606050215161502702314627266929  at 50 digits

the same number to TWENTY-NINE digits: a real singular value does not notice the working precision
at all.  The 9.1e-13 at X_0^1326(1) moved by a factor of 112 under exactly that change.  So the
precision test separates the two cases by some thirty orders of magnitude in sensitivity, and it
is the test to use whenever a residual stops responding to the cutoff.

Two explanations were tested and REFUTED before this one, and both are worth not repeating:

* the fixed `SCL := 10^16` rounding of the Gram matrix in nelson3.m.  Raising it to 10^(prec-4)
  (nelson3hi.m, stage3hi.m) changes the answer at X_0^1326(1) in NO digit at all -- 30 identical
  digits -- and likewise at all three controls.  The scaling only decides which lattice vectors
  the enumeration offers; each one is then accepted or rejected by `4 pi |X| <= cut` computed at
  full precision, so the rounding costs no accuracy.
* the Fourier-coefficient bound `nmax`.  Raising its margin from 20 to 4000 at X_0^57(1), and at
  X_0^1326(1) from 20 to 20000 -- coefficients to n = 37769 against the 17789 the cutoff asks for
  -- changes the residual in NO digit of the thirty, and the enumeration finds the same 59 352 084
  lattice points.  So nothing is being dropped, and the margin of 20 is ample (stage3x.m, NMX).

## X_0^6(403) and X_0^2418(1): two different curves with the same differentials

These two are easy to mistake for a duplicated row, and if they ever disagree it is easy to
mistake that for a bug.  They are neither.  Both have D*N = 2418, both carry the same
Atkin-Lehner group W = {1,2,13,26,93,186,1209,2418}, and both have genus 4; and in both cases the
space of differentials is spanned by the SAME four newforms of level exactly 2418 -- for
X_0^2418(1) because N = 1, and for X_0^6(403) because the W-fixed oldform part happens to vanish
there (nold.m says complete at both).  So their Jacobians are isogenous.

But they are quotients of different quaternionic curves: the evaluator is handed a different
quaternion algebra and a different Eichler order in the two cases, the points lie in different
fundamental domains, and the canonical images are different curves.

Hyperellipticity is not an isogeny invariant.  The witness is concrete: y^2 = x^8 + 1 is a
HYPERELLIPTIC curve of genus 3 carrying a Klein four group whose three quotients all have genus 1
and whose full quotient is rational -- genera 1, 1, 1 and 0, with the Kani-Rosen relation
3 + 2*0 = 1 + 1 + 1 -- which is exactly the shape that elsewhere in this project produces smooth
plane quartics.  A Jacobian that splits into elliptic curves says nothing about whether the curve
has a hyperelliptic involution.

So if these two come out differently, that is a legitimate result and not an inconsistency.  Do
not go looking for a defect on the strength of it.

## The cost law needs a precision clause

The README's cost section says the cutoff buys about 0.4 decimal digits per unit with no trend in
D*N.  That was measured at cutoffs 26 to 32 and at bases up to D*N = 4830, and it is right there.
It stops being right once the truncation error falls below the working precision: at
X_0^1326(1), 59 million lattice terms summed at 30 digits floor the residual at about 1e-12, and
cutoff 50 buys nothing over cutoff 46.  The controls do not show this because at X_0^35(1) and
X_0^57(1) the truncation error at cutoff 46 (4.3e-16, 5.1e-15) is still the larger of the two.
So: above roughly 10^7 lattice terms, raise PR with CUT, or the residual stalls.
